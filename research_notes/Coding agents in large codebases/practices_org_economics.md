# Working practices, organisational changes and economics for coding agents on large brownfield codebases

Research date is 2026-11-02. Each finding carries an evidence label, defined as follows.

- **[Measured]** means an experiment or telemetry from a party that does not sell the practice being tested.
- **[Vendor-measured]** means numbers from a party with a commercial interest in the result, usually without a published method.
- **[Field report]** means a first-person account of what a team did, without a comparison group.
- **[Opinion]** means a practitioner position or a conceptual model.
- **(snippet only)** means I saw the claim in a search result or secondary write-up and did not read the primary source, so it needs care before being repeated as fact.

## Techniques: which practices do serious field reports repeat, and what is the counter-evidence?

### Takeaway
The practices that recur across independent large-codebase reports are deterministic gates that the agent can run itself, fast isolated environments, tasks small enough to verify, and human review of every change, but almost all of the support is field report rather than controlled measurement. The one technique with direct controlled evidence, context files, shows a much weaker effect than practitioner enthusiasm suggests.

### Cited Findings

#### Harness engineering
- [Field report] OpenAI's harness post is dated 11 February 2026 and says a repository that began empty in late August 2025 held on the order of a million lines five months later, and it attributes slow early progress to an underspecified environment rather than to Codex — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/blog/harness-engineering) (snippet only, because the page returned 403 to direct fetch).
- [Field report] InfoQ's account says OpenAI enforced a fixed dependency order (Types, Config, Repo, Service, Runtime, UI) with mechanical rules and structural tests, and kept maps, execution plans and design specifications in a structured docs directory — [InfoQ, 21 Feb 2026](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/02/openai-harness-engineering-codex).
- [Field report] A secondary explainer reports about 1,500 pull requests over the build, and says a single large AGENTS.md failed and was replaced by a map of about 100 lines that points into `docs/` — [The Neuron, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/openais-harness-engineering-playbook-how-to-ship-1m-lines-of-code-without-writing-any/) (not confirmed against OpenAI's post).
- [Field report] Codex copied existing repository patterns, including poor ones, which caused drift over time — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/blog/harness-engineering) (snippet only).
- Counter-evidence: the OpenAI case started from an empty repository, so it is not a brownfield result, and InfoQ states no independent verification or criticism — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/02/openai-harness-engineering-codex).
- [Opinion] Birgitta Böckeler divides a harness into feedforward guides and feedback sensors, each either computational or inferential, and argues that feedback alone repeats mistakes while feedforward alone never checks that rules were followed — [martinfowler.com, 2 Apr 2026](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/harness-engineering.html).
- [Opinion] Böckeler says the harness is "most needed where it is hardest to build", because harnessability depends on properties such as strong typing and clear module boundaries that legacy code may lack, and she describes behavioural verification as the least solved category, with AI-generated tests "not good enough yet" — [martinfowler.com](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/harness-engineering.html). The article is a conceptual model and not a controlled study.
- [Opinion] Thoughtworks Radar Vol. 34 (April 2026) makes "Putting coding agents on a leash" one of its themes and names compilers, linters, type checkers and tests as deterministic feedback sensors — [Radar Vol. 34 press release](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20260415ae29507/); [Radar PDF](https://www.thoughtworks.com/content/dam/thoughtworks/documents/radar/2026/04/tr_technology_radar_vol_34_en_1.pdf) (snippet only for the PDF).
- [Field report] Anthropic's long-running agent harness uses an initializer agent, a JSON feature list with a `passes` field, one feature per session, and browser-driven end-to-end testing, and it reports qualitatively that browser testing "dramatically improved performance" — [Anthropic, 26 Nov 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents). The worked example is a new web application, there are no comparative metrics, and the authors call it "one possible set of solutions".

#### Research-plan-implement
- [Field report] HumanLayer's workflow separates research, planning and implementation, compacts state into files, targets 40-60% context utilisation, and implements in a git worktree — [HumanLayer ACE-FCA, about August 2025](https://github.com/humanlayer/advanced-context-engineering-for-coding-agents/blob/main/ace-fca.md).
- [Field report] On BAML, a Rust codebase of about 300k lines, the author fixed a bug in about an hour with no prior experience of the codebase, and with a BAML engineer produced about 35k lines in about 7 hours, against that engineer's estimate of 3-5 senior days per feature — [ACE-FCA](https://github.com/humanlayer/advanced-context-engineering-for-coding-agents/blob/main/ace-fca.md). The time comparison is an estimate, one resulting PR needed more work after merge, and the other was still open.
- Counter-evidence from the same source: a 7-hour attempt to remove Hadoop dependencies from parquet-java failed because research did not trace the dependency tree deeply enough, a two-week race-condition investigation hit dead ends, and the author says the approach probably is not right for most teams and that hard problems may need at least one codebase expert — [ACE-FCA](https://github.com/humanlayer/advanced-context-engineering-for-coding-agents/blob/main/ace-fca.md).
- Adjacent counter-evidence on heavyweight planning: Böckeler found Kiro expanded a small bug into four user stories with 16 acceptance criteria, said "I'd rather review code than all these markdown files", observed that agents frequently did not follow all instructions, and found that two of the three tools seemed even more work to introduce into an existing codebase — [martinfowler.com, 15 Oct 2025](https://www.martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html).
- [Opinion] Thoughtworks placed spec-driven development in Assess in November 2025 and warned that it may repeat a "bitter lesson" about handcrafted rules not scaling — [Thoughtworks Radar blip](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/spec-driven-development). The page says the blip is not on the current edition and that the assessment may have changed; the April 2026 Radar names Spec-Kit and OpenSpec as useful to its teams (snippet only) — [Radar PDF](https://www.thoughtworks.com/content/dam/thoughtworks/documents/radar/2026/04/tr_technology_radar_vol_34_en_1.pdf).

#### Context files (AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md)
- [Measured] Gloaguen et al. tested Claude Code, Codex and Qwen Code on 300 SWE-bench Lite tasks and on 138 tasks from 12 repositories that already had developer context files, and concluded that context files do "not generally improve task success rates" while raising cost by about 20-23% on average — [arXiv 2602.11988, v1 12 Feb 2026, v3 29 Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988).
- [Measured] In the same paper, LLM-generated files lowered resolution on the developer-context benchmark by about 2% and developer-written files raised it by about 2.4%, with neither reported as statistically significant for success, while step counts rose in every setting — [arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988). The per-model picture is mixed, for example Sonnet-4.5 went from 73.2% with no file to 70.3% with a developer file, and Qwen3-30B went from 45.7% to 53.6%.
- Source conflict: secondary write-ups quote a 4% gain for developer-written files — [DAIR.AI](https://academy.dair.ai/blog/agents-md-evaluation) (snippet only). The 2.4% comes from the current arXiv full text, and I checked that it matches the mean of the table values.
- Limits of that study: it covers Python only, measures only task resolution rather than code quality or security, samples each agent once per instance, and manually edited tests on 25 of the 138 tasks — [arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988). The authors still see value in context files for specifying non-standard practices — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988).
- [Measured, small and non-randomised description] Lulla et al. ran agents with and without AGENTS.md on 124 pull requests from 10 repositories and report an association with about 28.64% lower median runtime and about 16.58% lower output tokens, with comparable completion — [arXiv 2601.20404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20404). This points the opposite way on cost from Gloaguen et al., and the two studies use different samples and metrics.
- [Field report] Stripe's rule files are mostly conditional by subdirectory rather than global, because of the size of the codebase — [Stripe, 9 Feb 2026](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents).

#### Task sizing
- [Vendor-measured] Cognition says Devin does best on tasks that would take a junior engineer roughly 4-8 hours with clear requirements and verifiable outcomes, and does worse on ambiguous work or when requirements change mid-task — [Cognition, 14 Nov 2025](https://cognition.com/blog/devin-annual-performance-review-2025).
- [Field report] Anthropic observed that agents fail by attempting too much at once and by declaring work done too early, and addressed this with one feature per session — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents).
- [Field report] Kent Beck had two attempts become so complex that the agent stalled, and responded with one failing test at a time plus separate structural and behavioural changes — [Kent Beck, 25 Jun 2025](https://newsletter.kentbeck.com/p/augmented-coding-beyond-the-vibes).
- Counter-evidence: Cursor reports a three-week Solid-to-React migration in its own codebase at +266K/-193K edits, far larger than any recommended task size, though it says the work passed CI and early checks and still needed careful review — [Cursor, 14 Jan 2026](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents).

#### Deterministic guardrails and fast feedback
- [Field report] Stripe interleaves deterministic steps with agent reasoning and limits a minion to two CI rounds before it stops — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents); local lint is reported as kept under five seconds — [InfoQ, March 2026](https://infoq.com/news/2026/03/stripe-autonomous-coding-agents/) (snippet only).
- [Field report] Spotify wraps each Honk coding task in a verification loop of linting, compiling and testing — [Spotify engineering, April 2026](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/4/background-coding-agents-dataset-migrations-honk-part-4) (snippet only).
- [Measured, survey-based] DORA 2025 (nearly 5,000 respondents) describes AI as an amplifier and says that without strong automated testing, mature version control and fast feedback, rising change volume produces instability — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report?e=48754805) (snippet only). This concerns AI assistance in general and is correlational.
- Counter-evidence: Faros says its 2026 quality effects appear regardless of baseline engineering maturity, which conflicts with the amplifier thesis — [Faros research](https://faros.ai/research/ai-acceleration-whiplash) (snippet only).
- Known failure mode: Beck lists disabling or deleting tests as a warning sign of an agent going off track, and a reader of his post reports that agents ignored TDD instructions until he externalised the phase in a file — [Kent Beck](https://newsletter.kentbeck.com/p/augmented-coding-beyond-the-vibes).

#### Sandboxed execution
- [Field report] Stripe's pre-warmed devboxes spin up in about 10 seconds — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents), and Ramp reports under 5 seconds for remote sandboxes — [Pragmatic Engineer, 25 Aug 2026](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/why-ramp-built-inspect).
- [Vendor-measured] Anthropic says OS-level sandboxing reduces permission prompts by 84% in internal usage, gives no method, and argues that filesystem and network isolation must be combined — [Anthropic, 20 Oct 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-sandboxing). This measures interruptions and does not measure code outcomes.

#### Parallel agents and worktrees
- [Opinion, first-person] Simon Willison runs several agents in separate checkouts but says "I can only focus on reviewing and landing one significant change at a time", so he parallelises research, codebase explanation, small maintenance, carefully specified work and scouting — [Willison, 5 Oct 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/5/parallel-coding-agents/).
- [Opinion, first-person] Armin Ronacher runs parallel agents less than before because "it's only so much my mind can review" — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/new-trend-programming-by-kicking?triedRedirect=true) (snippet only).
- [Field report] Ramp found that local tools limited engineers to one or two sessions, which was one of its reasons for moving to remote sandboxes with unlimited concurrent sessions — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/why-ramp-built-inspect).
- [Measured] METR warns that developers running several agents often worked on unrelated tasks while waiting, which made self-reported time on task unreliable — [METR, 24 Feb 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/).
- [Opinion, with cost anecdote] Steve Yegge's Gas Town orchestrates many agents with roles and merge queues, was released on 1 January 2026, and one early adopter reported about $100 per hour — [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/background/Full-Control-Gas-Town-Orchestrates-Ten-or-More-Coding-Agents-11178824.html?view=print); [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/steve-yegges-ai-agent-orchestration-project-gas-town-comes-to-the-cloud-and-brings-the-wasteland-with-it/) (both snippet only). A sceptical view argues that no product can or should grow as fast as Gas Town goes — [Benn Stancil](https://benn.substack.com/p/gas-town) (snippet only).

#### Subagents, and multi-agent versus single-agent
- [Opinion] Cognition's June 2025 essay says parallel subagents build on conflicting assumptions and recommends a single-threaded agent, under the principles "share full agent traces" and "actions carry implicit decisions" — [Cognition, 12 Jun 2025](https://cognition.com/blog/dont-build-multi-agents).
- [Vendor-measured and field report] Cognition's April 2026 follow-up keeps that objection to parallel writers but endorses setups where one agent writes and others contribute intelligence, reports clean-context review catching an average of 2 bugs per PR with roughly 58% severe, and describes manager Devins that split work among children, with no performance numbers — [Cognition, 22 Apr 2026](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working).
- [Field report] The same follow-up reports two limits: a cheap primary model escalating to a stronger model did not work well enough because the primary could not tell when to escalate, and managers without deep codebase context were overly prescriptive — [Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working).
- Counter-position: Cursor ran hundreds of concurrent workers pushing to one branch, using planners, sub-planners, workers and a judge, and built over a million lines of a browser in about a week — [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents). Cursor also reports that flat self-coordination reduced 20 agents to the throughput of two or three, that an integrator role added bottlenecks, and that periodic fresh starts remain necessary.
- Quality check on that counter-position: Willison built the browser and found it working but flawed, with rendering glitches and pages legible and mostly correct — [Willison, 19 Jan 2026](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jan/19/scaling-long-running-autonomous-coding/) (snippet only).
- Anthropic says it is unclear whether a single general agent or a multi-agent architecture is best — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents).
- [Field report, third-party description] Amp treats a subagent as a mini-Amp with its own context window and uses a code-search subagent over a Sourcegraph index — [Amp write-up](https://rhizome-lab.github.io/moss/research/ampcode.html); [Vaughan, 7 Apr 2026](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/04/07/sourcegraph-amp-multi-repo-orchestrator/) (both snippet only and neither is an Amp primary source).

#### Repository readiness and refactoring for agents
- [Vendor-affiliated, peer-reviewed] Borg et al. had LLMs refactor 5,000 Python files and found an association between the CodeHealth metric and whether meaning survived refactoring — [arXiv 2601.02200, FORGE 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02200). The files come from competitive programming, so they do not represent brownfield systems, and CodeScene co-authors sell the metric.
- Source conflict: CodeScene's press release headlines a 30% defect-risk increase in unhealthy code — [press release, 28 Jan 2026](https://tools.prnewswire.com/en-us/live/20823/release/20260128EN71904) (snippet only), its playbook cites 60% — [CodeScene playbook](https://codescene.com/hubfs/CodeScene-AI-Playbook-2026.pdf) (snippet only), and the study covered only code scoring 7.0 or above, so risk below that is a projection — [CodeScene whitepaper](https://codescene.com/hubfs/whitepapers/AI-Ready-Code-How-Code-Health-Determines-AI-Performance.pdf) (snippet only).
- [Field report] Stripe says its Ruby stack with Sorbet and homegrown libraries is unknown to LLMs, which it gives as a reason for custom tooling — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents).

#### Large-codebase production reports
- [Field report] Stripe merges over a thousand completely minion-produced PRs per week across hundreds of millions of lines, and humans review every PR — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents). Later coverage cites 1,300 per week — [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/03/stripe-autonomous-coding-agents/) (snippet only). The share of all Stripe PRs and the defect rate are not stated in what I read.
- [Field report] About 75% of Ramp's merged PRs come from Inspect sessions, up from about 60% in January — [Pragmatic Engineer, 25 Aug 2026](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/why-ramp-built-inspect). This counts sessions as origin and does not show how much human steering each PR received.
- [Vendor-measured] Devin's merge rate rose from 34% to 67% in a year, a large bank migrated proprietary ETL files in 3-4 hours against 30-40 for humans, and Java version migration took 14x less time per repository — [Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/devin-annual-performance-review-2025).
- [Measured, third party] Amplifying.ai measured a 60.3% merge rate on public GitHub activity (38,374 of 63,599 PRs, 22 June to 16 August 2026), with 41.7% of merged PRs in significant repositories receiving human review — [Amplifying.ai](https://amplifying.ai/coding-agents/devin) (snippet only). Public repositories are not the enterprise population, and merge rate is affected by task selection — [AgentMarketCap, 13 Jul 2026](https://agentmarketcap.ai/blog/2026/07/13/cognition-devin-67-percent-pr-merge-rate-vendor-production-metric) (snippet only).

### Inferences
- The strongest recurring pattern is the combination of narrow tasks, an environment the agent can verify in, and mandatory human review, because Stripe, Ramp, Spotify and Cognition describe it independently. These are all self-reports by parties who built or sell the system, so the convergence is suggestive rather than proof.
- The successful migration reports (bank ETL, Java upgrades, Spotify dataset migrations, Solid-to-React) share repetitive structure with a mechanical success check. They should not be extrapolated to ambiguous product work on the same codebases.
- Gloaguen et al. and Lulla et al. can both be true if context files shorten some runs and add exploration in others, so the practical reading is short files holding non-obvious conventions, measured before being scaled. This reading is mine, and neither paper tests file length directly.
- Cognition's movement from "don't" to "one writer, many readers" and Cursor's hierarchy converge on avoiding uncoordinated writers. The real remaining dispute is whether many writers under strict hierarchy can produce code good enough to keep, which the browser experiment does not settle because it was a greenfield demonstration.
- The prominent harness and multi-agent reports (OpenAI's repository, Cursor's browser, Anthropic's web app) are greenfield, while the brownfield reports (Stripe, Ramp, Spotify) rely on heavy prior investment in developer platforms. This limits their transfer to organisations without such platforms.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study comparing a harnessed against an unharnessed setup on a large brownfield repository, and no controlled study of research-plan-implement, task sizing or worktrees.
- I could not read OpenAI's post directly (403), so team size and the 1,500 PR figure rest on secondary coverage.
- I found no primary Amp or Sourcegraph large-codebase report with numbers. What exists in my results is third-party description and a podcast listing.
- Whether "golden principles" cleanup agents appear in OpenAI's own text remains unconfirmed.
- I did not find a reliable independent measurement of sandboxing or subagents on code outcomes.

## Organisation: what rollout, role, accountability and metric changes do companies report?

### Takeaway
Companies are moving away from measuring AI usage as a performance signal, with Meta and Amazon both publicly retreating in the autumn of 2026, while peer-led rollout is widely recommended but has no controlled comparison against mandates. Dedicated internal teams now own agent platforms at some leading companies, and the review queue has become the most clearly measured organisational bottleneck.

### Cited Findings

#### Rollout, champions and mandates
- [Field report] LeadDev describes IBM starting with its most "AI ready" teams and declining to mandate because forced use risks "shallow adoption", and Carta using weekly office hours, show-and-tell, per-tool Slack channels and a technical-debt refactoring hackathon, with token dashboards used to find power users — [LeadDev, 9 Feb 2026](https://leaddev.com/ai/ai-champions-are-the-key-to-engineering-adoption).
- [Field report] HubSpot reportedly reached 100% engineer adoption without mandates and a 73% rise in code lines updated — [Dealroom news feed](https://dummy.dealroom.co/news/feed/hubspot-achieves-100-engineer-ai-adoption-without-mandates-boosting-code-updates-73) (snippet only, and lines updated is a weak outcome measure).
- [Field report] A trainer of over 700 bank engineers says a leadership mandate buys compliance during the workshop and reversion the week after — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/crimsonmed/i-trained-700-engineers-on-ai-the-tool-was-never-the-problem-lfe) (snippet only, anecdotal).
- Counter-evidence: Uber's adoption rose quickly, from about one-third in February to 84% in March — [Techstrong, 26 May 2026](https://techstrong.ai/agentic-ai/ai-spending-vs-productivity-uber-coo-sounds-alarm-on-murky-returns/), and fast adoption did not by itself establish product benefit, according to Uber's own COO in the same article.
- [Survey] Staff+ engineers report the highest agent use at 63.5%, ahead of directors/VPs at 51.9%, engineers at 49.7% and managers at 46.1%, among 906 self-selected Pragmatic Engineer subscribers — [Pragmatic Engineer, 3 Mar 2026](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/ai-tooling-2026).
- [Survey] Copilot leads at companies with 10,000+ employees at 56%, and the author suggests that procurement rather than preference drives the difference — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/ai-tooling-2026).
- [Opinion, disputed] Yegge claimed that Google's adoption follows an industry curve of a small group of agentic power users, a similar group of refusers and a majority on chat tools, and Google's Addy Osmani replied that over 40,000 Google engineers use agentic coding weekly — [Willison, 13 Apr 2026](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/13/steve-yegge/). Weekly use and power use are different measures, so the two statements do not directly contradict.

#### AI usage in performance reviews, and metrics
- [Press report of internal memo] Meta will no longer count AI dashboards and token counters in reviews and will judge quality, speed and complexity of work, after engineers competed on internal leaderboards, with internal use described as heading toward billions in 2026 and budgets planned from 2027 — [The Decoder, 8 Sep 2026](https://the-decoder.com/meta-drops-ai-usage-from-engineer-performance-reviews-after-tokenmaxxing-backfires/).
- [Primary memo via press] Amazon's memo, published 27 October 2026, calls token usage, adoption and counts of code changes "deeply flawed" when used as targets, yet says they correlate with outcomes and can be used "with care and judgement", and asks managers to study the bottom 10% of teams by adoption and check whether the top 10% deliver more — [TNW, 28 Oct 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/amazon-tokenmaxxing-memo-engineers-ai-metrics).
- [Press] AWS VP Marc Brooker told CNBC that Amazon looks at feature velocity, meaning launches and successful production changes — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/amazon-tokenmaxxing-memo-engineers-ai-metrics).
- Nuance: Carta's use of token dashboards to find people to learn from is a different use from ranking people for reviews — [LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/ai/ai-champions-are-the-key-to-engineering-adoption).

#### Role changes and platform ownership
- [Field report] Ramp's Inspect team has 5.5 people (four engineers, a director and a part-time PM), and more than 150 engineers have contributed to the codebase — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/why-ramp-built-inspect).
- [Field report] Spotify ties Honk to its Backstage developer platform and Fleet Management, with Core Infrastructure named as running background agents, though the sources do not explicitly say that a platform team owns the harness — [Spotify engineering](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/4/background-coding-agents-dataset-migrations-honk-part-4); [Spotify webinar](https://backstage.spotify.com/how-spotify-built-honk) (both snippet only).
- [Opinion] Böckeler describes the human's job as steering by improving the harness whenever an issue recurs — [martinfowler.com](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/harness-engineering.html).
- [Survey with quotes] Pragmatic Engineer sorts engineers into builders, shippers and coasters, says AI amplifies the existing type, reports builders frustrated by higher-volume and lower-signal review, and suggests managers may become more hands-on — [repost of Pragmatic Engineer, 27 Apr 2026](https://the-pm-archive.vercel.app/articles/2026-04-27-the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineers-in-2026-key-trends). This is an unofficial mirror, so it should be checked against the original.

#### Junior hiring
- [Press] IBM is reported to be tripling US entry-level hiring in 2026 after rewriting junior job descriptions — [search result citing IBM's CHRO](https://www.aol.com/news/7-companies-increasing-hiring-entry-103301833.html) (snippet only; this page returned 404 when I fetched it, so the claim needs another source).
- [Measured, secondhand] Stanford Digital Economy Lab research is cited for a nearly 20% employment decline among software developers aged 22-25 from the late-2022 peak — [AI2 Work summary](https://ai2.work/blog/stanford-index-junior-coder-jobs-fall-20-as-ai-reshapes-hiring) (snippet only). Causation by AI is contested in this line of work, and macro factors overlap with the period.
- [Measured, secondhand] Indeed data is cited for senior postings up 13.5% and entry-level postings down 6.3% since January 2025, while TrueUp data is cited for software openings up 30% in 2026 — [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/software-engineering-openings-jump-30-even-as-52-000-tech-workers-lose-jobs/) (snippet only; the Indeed figure's page was not individually confirmed).
- [Field report] HumanLayer reports an intern shipping 2 PRs on the first day and 10 by the eighth, and also says hard problems may need a codebase expert — [ACE-FCA](https://github.com/humanlayer/advanced-context-engineering-for-coding-agents/blob/main/ace-fca.md).

#### Ownership, review and accountability
- [Field report] Stripe requires that an engineer opens each minion PR and another engineer reviews it — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents).
- [Field report, opposite direction] OpenAI's team relied largely on agent review, with Codex self-review followed by other agent reviewers — [InfoQ/explainer coverage of OpenAI](https://www.theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/openais-harness-engineering-playbook-how-to-ship-1m-lines-of-code-without-writing-any/) (snippet only).
- [Vendor-measured] Organisations with high AI code generation wait 105% longer for first review than those with low levels, with the median rising from 40 minutes at 20-40% AI code to 82 minutes at 80%+ — [DX Q3 2026 report](https://getdx.com/report/state-of-ai-impact-in-engineering-q3-report/). The absolute waits are still short.
- [Vendor-measured] Faros reports that median time in review rose 441.5% and PRs merged with no review rose 31.3% between low and high adoption periods — [Faros, 12 Apr 2026](https://faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways).

### Inferences
- The reversals at Meta and Amazon are a textbook case of a measure degrading once it becomes a target, but Amazon has not abandoned usage data, so the emerging position is usage as a diagnostic and not as a score.
- Ramp's team of 5.5 gives one concrete size for a harness team at a company that writes most of its PRs through the system. It should not be read as a general benchmark.
- The Stripe and OpenAI positions on review represent a real policy difference. Stripe's has higher stakes and a brownfield setting, which is a plausible reason for the difference, and regulated firms such as capital markets organisations would more naturally resemble Stripe.
- The mandate debate is likely mis-framed, because IBM's and Uber's cases suggest adoption is easy to obtain and that the harder question is converting it into outcomes.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled comparison of mandated and voluntary rollout, and no measured evidence on the effect of training programmes.
- Reports that Shopify and Microsoft factor AI use into reviews, and that Amazon managers still look at the numbers according to anonymous Financial Times sources, appeared only in search summaries, and I did not confirm them.
- A randomised Anthropic study of 52 engineers scoring 17% lower when learning a library with AI appeared only in a secondary summary and was not verified. It would matter for the junior pipeline if confirmed.
- I found no formal company data on who is accountable for agent-caused incidents, beyond review policies. The claim of about 120,000 lost orders in an Amazon outage appears in DX's article without a source, so it should not be used without one.
- I found no reliable count of companies with a named agent platform team.

## Economics: what do agents really cost per engineer, and is there return on investment?

### Takeaway
The credible per-engineer range is wide, with a reported average of about $150-250 per month at Uber, heavy users around $2,000, and caps now appearing at $1,500 per tool per month, and the move from flat seats to token billing has made the tail the main budget risk. Return-on-investment evidence remains weak, with telemetry showing throughput increases, conflicting quality signals, and a senior operator at Uber saying the link to product value is not there yet.

### Cited Findings

#### Per-engineer spend
- [Press, executive statements] Uber's CTO said the 2026 AI coding budget was exhausted within about four months, the average bill was $150-250 per month, heavy users reached up to $2,000, and he used $1,200 in tokens in two hours in one demonstration — [Techstrong, 26 May 2026](https://techstrong.ai/agentic-ai/ai-spending-vs-productivity-uber-coo-sounds-alarm-on-murky-returns/). The total budget is undisclosed.
- [Press, spokesperson] Uber now limits spend to $1,500 per person per tool per month, with a dashboard and an exception process, according to Bloomberg on 2 June 2026 — [GIGAZINE, 5 Jun 2026](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260605-uber-caps-ai). Willison estimates two tools at about $36,000 a year, or roughly 11% of a median US software engineer salary of about $330,000, per the same article.
- Source conflict: sources disagree on when Uber's budget ran out (GIGAZINE notes "early 2026" versus by April), on adoption (84% versus 95%), and on the earlier agent-code share (below 1% versus a few percent), while about 11% of backend updates is consistent — [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260605-uber-caps-ai); [Techstrong](https://techstrong.ai/agentic-ai/ai-spending-vs-productivity-uber-coo-sounds-alarm-on-murky-returns/); [KuCoin news flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/uber-cto-reveals-ai-coding-tools-exceeded-annual-budget-11-of-backend-code-now-ai-written) (snippet only). The Information's original is paywalled.
- [Survey] Pragmatic Engineer reports company-paid tools at roughly $100-200 per month per engineer, about 30% of respondents regularly hitting limits, and a CPTO paying $600 per month personally against a team plan of about $100 — [repost, 27 Apr 2026](https://the-pm-archive.vercel.app/articles/2026-04-27-the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineers-in-2026-key-trends).
- [Analyst, secondhand] Gartner research reportedly finds nearly a quarter of technology leaders spending $200-500 per developer per month on tokens — [DevOps.com](https://devops.com/ai-coding-costs-could-exceed-developer-salaries-gartner-warns/) (snippet only; direct fetch returned 403).
- [Field report] HumanLayer's team of three averaged about $12k per month on Opus, which works out at about $4,000 per engineer per month, for a team that is itself an agent-tool company — [ACE-FCA](https://github.com/humanlayer/advanced-context-engineering-for-coding-agents/blob/main/ace-fca.md).
- [Vendor telemetry] Vantage's index showed a median of $23.71 and an average of $149.98 across 25,944 developers for a partial August 2026 (snippet only), and for November 2026 it shows a median of $2.11 after only one day of data — [Vantage](https://tokens.vantage.sh/typical-spend). The population is self-selected API users, so it is not an enterprise benchmark, though the gap between mean and median is informative.
- Source conflict: The range of $150-3,000 per developer per month across 27 workplaces is attributed to Pragmatic Engineer by secondary write-ups, which call it anecdotal — [search summary source](https://engineered.at/articles/the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineers-in-2026-key-trends) (snippet only, not confirmed in the original).

#### Pricing changes and tool consolidation
- [Primary] GitHub moved Copilot to usage-based AI Credits on 1 June 2026, saying the premium request model "is no longer sustainable" because agentic use has higher inference demand. Business stays at $19 and Enterprise at $39 per seat with matching included credits, pooled usage and budget controls at enterprise, cost centre and user level, and promotional credits of $30 and $70 for June to August — [GitHub, 27 Apr 2026](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/).
- [Press report of internal memo] Microsoft's Experiences and Devices group is cancelling most internal Claude Code licences by the end of June and moving to Copilot CLI. The memo gives product and security reasons, while unnamed sources cite fiscal year-end cost — [The Decoder, 15 May 2026](https://the-decoder.com/microsoft-pulls-claude-code-licenses-and-pushes-developers-back-toward-its-own-ai-tool/). No dollar figures appear in that article, and the widely repeated figure of about $2,000 per engineer per month is not confirmed. Microsoft also owns the alternative product, so this is not a clean cost signal.
- [Press] Meta plans budgets and a central usage dashboard from 2027 — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/meta-drops-ai-usage-from-engineer-performance-reviews-after-tokenmaxxing-backfires/).

#### Build versus buy
- [Field report] Ramp built on OpenCode because local tools allowed only one or two sessions, because it wanted designers to make small UI changes, and because engineers needed remote environments for cross-service debugging — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/why-ramp-built-inspect).
- [Field report] Stripe built because its stack is unusual and its stakes are high, on a fork of Block's Goose — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents); [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/03/stripe-autonomous-coding-agents/) (fork detail snippet only).
- [Field report] Spotify took a hybrid path, wrapping Anthropic's Agent SDK in its own harness and integrating internal orchestration — [Spotify](https://backstage.spotify.com/how-spotify-built-honk) (snippet only).
- [Opinion] Commentators advise buying unless integration requirements exceed what any commercial tool can meet, or buying the agent loop and building the private context layer — [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/build-vs-buy-ai-coding-agents-and-how-to-measure-the-return-on-investment); [Saffari](https://omidsaffari.com/blog/build-vs-buy-coding-agents-for-internal-workflows-2026) (snippet only).

#### Model routing
- [Field report] Some practitioners plan with Opus and execute with a cheaper model, and one European firm trains new hires on which model to use for which task — [repost of Pragmatic Engineer](https://the-pm-archive.vercel.app/articles/2026-04-27-the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineers-in-2026-key-trends).
- [Vendor-measured] Not Diamond reports cost savings rising from 39% to 66% when open-weight models join its pool, and also says a router should reliably deliver at least 10-20% — [Not Diamond](https://www.notdiamond.ai/blog/not-diamond-code-intelligent-model-routing-for-coding-agents) (snippet only). This company sells routing.
- Counter-evidence: mis-routes create rework according to one estimate of 30-40% savings — [Unblocked](https://getunblocked.com/blog/model-routing-coding-agents) (snippet only), and Cognition found that automatic escalation from a cheap primary did not work well enough because the primary could not judge when to escalate — [Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working).
- Counter-evidence on model choice: Pragmatic Engineer notes that a cheaper default model could worsen the experience for some engineers — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/ai-tooling-2026). Variance also makes single-run comparisons unreliable, as Arize notes — [Arize](https://arize.com/blog/how-i-cut-coding-agent-costs-with-model-harness-routing/) (snippet only).

#### Return on investment
- [Vendor telemetry] DX found median PR throughput up 7.76%, mean 13.1%, 90th percentile about 44%, with most organisations at 5-15%, across 400+ companies from November 2024 to February 2026, while AI usage rose 65% — [DX, 28 Apr 2026](https://getdx.com/news/new-data-ais-impact-on-engineering-velocity-is-more-modest-than-expected/).
- [Vendor telemetry] DX's Q3 2026 report covers 500+ companies and shows median weekly TrueThroughput rising from 1.43 to 2.51 weighted PRs per developer between Q4 2025 and Q3 2026, which is the stated 75.5%, and it gives no defect or change failure numbers — [DX Q3 2026](https://getdx.com/report/state-of-ai-impact-in-engineering-q3-report/). This is complexity-weighted PR volume and not business value.
- [Vendor telemetry] Faros compares each organisation's lowest and highest adoption periods across about 22,000 developers and reports epics per developer +66%, task throughput +33.7%, bugs per developer +54%, incidents per PR +242.7%, and monthly incidents +57.9% — [Faros](https://faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways). The comparison is observational, Faros sells governance products, and it says the 861% churn-ratio rise could have several causes.
- [Measured] METR's February 2026 update reports -18% task time (CI -38% to +9%) for 10 returning developers and -4% (CI -15% to +9%) for 47 new developers, compared with +19% in early 2025. METR calls the new data unreliable due to selection, believes the true speedup is likely higher, and says the data is only very weak evidence — [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/). Some coverage misreports this as a flat reversal.
- [Executive statement] Uber COO Andrew Macdonald said of the link between usage and consumer benefit, "That link is not there yet", and said that trading headcount for unproven technology is harder to justify — [Techstrong](https://techstrong.ai/agentic-ai/ai-spending-vs-productivity-uber-coo-sounds-alarm-on-murky-returns/).
- [Survey] European companies generally want clear ROI before increasing spend while US companies invest first, and one CTO persuaded a CFO using lost productivity when engineers hit limits rather than productivity gains — [repost of Pragmatic Engineer](https://the-pm-archive.vercel.app/articles/2026-04-27-the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineers-in-2026-key-trends).

### Inferences
- A rough break-even can be derived using salary alone, which understates loaded cost: an engineer at about $200 per month costs about $2,400 a year, or under 1% of a $330,000 salary, while two tools at the Uber cap cost about 11%. DX's typical 5-15% throughput band would comfortably cover the average user but only roughly cover a capped heavy user, assuming that PR throughput converts to value, which is the unproven step.
- The budget problem is mainly a distribution problem, since Uber's average is far below its cap and Vantage's mean is several times its median. Governance therefore focuses on the tail, and per-tool caps with exception processes are one observed response.
- DX's 7.76% and 75.5% figures are not directly comparable, since they cover different windows and samples. The difference may reflect stronger models after early 2026, which DX's benchmarks post suggests — [DX benchmarks](https://getdx.com/blog/2026-dx-benchmarks-are-now-available/) (snippet only), but it may also reflect sample or metric changes.
- Build decisions in the reports are justified by concurrency and by integration with internal systems, rather than by token savings. None of the reports I read presents a cost comparison.

### Gaps
- I found no audited token or seat spend per engineer at a large company. The Uber figures are the most specific, and they come from press reports of executive statements.
- Direct fetches of the Forbes piece (403) and DevOps.com Gartner article (403) failed, and The Information's and Bloomberg's originals were not available to me. Their numbers are secondhand here.
- I found no independent controlled evidence of savings from model routing on a large enterprise population.
- I found no credible ROI study that links agent use to revenue, feature value or customer outcomes.
- Ramp's and Stripe's build costs are not disclosed, so total cost of ownership cannot be compared with buying.
- Claims that Claude Code token consumption rose 4x-10x on identical tasks from 31 March 2026 and that 80% of companies exceed budgets appeared only on low-quality vendor blogs, so I excluded them from the findings.

## Evidence versus opinion: which practices are supported, and where do credible practitioners disagree?

### Takeaway
Controlled or large-sample evidence exists for only a few questions (context files, overall speedup, throughput and review wait, and quality trends), and much of the rest is well-informed field report or opinion from parties with a commercial interest. The most useful disagreements concern review policy, parallel writers, how much process to use, and whether AI's quality impact depends on prior engineering maturity.

#### Credible disagreements
- Whether code should remain under human review: Stripe keeps humans reviewing every PR — [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents), while OpenAI's account leans on agent review — [The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/openais-harness-engineering-playbook-how-to-ship-1m-lines-of-code-without-writing-any/) (snippet only), and Willison treats his review capacity as the limiting factor — [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/5/parallel-coding-agents/).
- Whether code quality still matters directly: Beck says that in augmented coding "you care about the code, its complexity, the tests, & their coverage" — [Beck](https://newsletter.kentbeck.com/p/augmented-coding-beyond-the-vibes), whereas Yegge's Gas Town work pursues many agents and high throughput — [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/steve-yegges-ai-agent-orchestration-project-gas-town-comes-to-the-cloud-and-brings-the-wasteland-with-it/) (snippet only). Beck's post dates from June 2025 and may not reflect his current view.
- Whether many agents should write at once: Cognition says parallel-writer swarms see little adoption — [Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working), while Cursor reports hundreds of concurrent workers — [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents). Cursor also reports that prompts mattered more than the harness or models, which conflicts with the emphasis others place on the harness.
- How much process to use: HumanLayer argues for explicit research and planning — [ACE-FCA](https://github.com/humanlayer/advanced-context-engineering-for-coding-agents/blob/main/ace-fca.md), whereas Böckeler found spec tools heavy for small work and hard to introduce to existing code — [martinfowler.com](https://www.martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html). The two are compatible if process scales with task size.
- Whether context files help: the results of [Gloaguen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988) conflict with those of [Lulla et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20404) on cost, and practitioners at [Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents) continue to rely on scoped rule files.
- Whether strong foundations protect quality: DORA says AI amplifies existing strengths — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report?e=48754805) (snippet only), while Faros says its effects appear regardless of maturity — [Faros research](https://faros.ai/research/ai-acceleration-whiplash) (snippet only).
- Whether productivity gains are real at the outcome level: DX and Faros show rising throughput — [DX](https://getdx.com/report/state-of-ai-impact-in-engineering-q3-report/); [Faros](https://faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways), METR cannot establish a speedup with confidence — [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/), and Uber's COO does not yet see a consumer benefit link — [Techstrong](https://techstrong.ai/agentic-ai/ai-spending-vs-productivity-uber-coo-sounds-alarm-on-murky-returns/).

### Inferences
- Nearly all of the telemetry comes from firms selling engineering analytics, and nearly all of the technique reports come from firms selling agents or from teams promoting their own platforms. No neutral party has yet run a large brownfield comparison, which is the largest weakness in this body of evidence.
- The defensible advice is therefore to adopt gates, isolation and review because they carry little downside and predate AI, and to treat context-file growth, routing and parallel writers as experiments to be measured locally.
- Capital markets readers have the most relevant examples in Stripe's human-review posture and Cognition's bank ETL migration, while neither example gives independent evidence on regulated production quality.

### Gaps
- I could not locate any neutral, controlled, multi-company study of agent practices on large proprietary codebases.
- The positions of Beck, Willison and Yegge are represented here by a limited number of posts, some more than a year old, and their current views may differ.
- I did not review conference talks in this pass, so talks by Stripe, Spotify, and Dex Horthy are represented only through written material.
