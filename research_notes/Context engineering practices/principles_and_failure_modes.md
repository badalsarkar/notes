# Context Engineering: Definition, Principles and Failure Modes

Research date: 2026-10-26. Every finding notes the source date. Results are labeled "measured" when they come from a benchmark or controlled experiment and "vendor claim" or "practitioner report" when they come from a company's own product or an engineering write-up without a published method.

## 1. How do Anthropic, LangChain, Karpathy, Lutke, Cognition, Manus, Google and OpenAI define and frame context engineering?

### Takeaway
All of these sources describe context engineering as the ongoing, system-level job of deciding which tokens reach the model on each call. That covers instructions, tools, retrieved data, memory and message history. Prompt engineering is described as one part of it, the part that writes the instructions. The sources differ mainly in what they emphasize. Anthropic and Google emphasize minimal, scoped context. LangChain gives a taxonomy of operations. Cognition warns about context fragmentation across agents. Manus focuses on KV-cache economics.

### Cited Findings
- **Tobi Lutke (Shopify CEO), X post of 18 June 2025.** He wrote: "I really like the term 'context engineering' over prompt engineering. It describes the core skill better: the art of providing all the context for the task to be plausibly solvable by the LLM." — [Simon Willison, 27 Jun 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/jun/27/context-engineering/); [original post](https://twitter.com/tobi/status/1935533422589399127)
- **Andrej Karpathy, X post of about 25 June 2025.** He wrote "+1 for 'context engineering' over 'prompt engineering'." He contrasted the term with "prompts," which people associate with short task descriptions, and called context engineering "the delicate art and science of filling the context window with just the right information for the next step." Secondary sources give different dates for the post. — [Simon Willison, 27 Jun 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/jun/27/context-engineering/); [original post](https://twitter.com/karpathy/status/1937902205765607626)
- **Karpathy's operating-system analogy.** LangChain cites his framing of the LLM as a CPU and the context window as RAM, the model's limited working memory. — [LangChain, 2 Jul 2025](https://www.langchain.com/blog/context-engineering-for-agents)
- **Simon Willison's view on the term.** "Prompt engineering" came to be read as typing things into a chatbot. Willison expects the meaning people infer from "context engineering" to sit much closer to what its proponents intend, and he gives that as the reason the term is more likely to last. — [Simon Willison, 27 Jun 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/jun/27/context-engineering/)
- **Cognition (Walden Yan), "Don't Build Multi-Agents", 12 June 2025.** Context engineering is the next level of prompt engineering, done "automatically in a dynamic system." Yan calls it effectively the most important job of engineers building AI agents. — [Cognition, 12 Jun 2025](https://cognition.com/blog/dont-build-multi-agents)
  - Principle 1 reads "Share context, and share full agent traces, not just individual messages."
  - Principle 2 reads "Actions carry implicit decisions, and conflicting decisions carry bad results."
  - The Flappy Bird example shows the problem. Two parallel subagents receive the same task, and one builds a Super Mario-style background while the other builds a bird that does not match it. The final agent is then left to reconcile outputs that do not fit together.
  - Yan recommends a single-threaded linear agent by default. For very long tasks he suggests a separate model that compresses history into key details, events and decisions, and Cognition fine-tuned smaller models for this job. He acknowledges that this approach also reaches a limit eventually.
  - He notes that, as of June 2025, Claude Code subagents never run in parallel with the main agent and mostly answer well-defined questions rather than write code. Their benefit is that investigative work stays out of the main agent's history.
- **LangChain, "Context Engineering for Agents", 2 July 2025.** The post defines context engineering as filling the context window with the right information at each step of an agent's trajectory. It groups the strategies into four operations, Write, Select, Compress and Isolate (see Section 3). — [LangChain, 2 Jul 2025](https://www.langchain.com/blog/context-engineering-for-agents)
- **Manus (Yichao "Peak" Ji), "Context Engineering for AI Agents: Lessons from Building Manus", 18 July 2025.** This is a practitioner report from production experience. — [Manus, 18 Jul 2025](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)
  - Ji writes that "the KV-cache hit rate is the single most important metric for a production-stage AI agent."
  - The average input-to-output token ratio in Manus is about 100:1. With Claude Sonnet, cached input costs 0.30 USD per million tokens and uncached input costs 3 USD per million tokens, a tenfold difference. A typical task takes about 50 tool calls.
  - The lessons are to keep prefixes stable and context append-only, and to mask tool logits rather than add or remove tools mid-task ("unless absolutely necessary, avoid dynamically adding or removing tools mid-iteration").
  - Manus uses the file system as unlimited, restorable memory. A web page can be dropped from context as long as its URL is kept.
  - The agent recites its plan by rewriting todo.md, which keeps the goal near the end of the context. This counters goal drift and lost-in-the-middle effects.
  - Failed actions and error traces stay in context so the model can update its beliefs.
  - Controlled variation in serialization avoids few-shot mimicry ("don't few-shot yourself into a rut"). In one example, an agent reviewing 20 resumes fell into a repetitive rhythm.
- **Anthropic, "Effective context engineering for AI agents", 29 September 2025.** — [Anthropic Engineering, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
  - Context is "the set of tokens included when sampling from an LLM." Context engineering is "curating and maintaining the optimal set of tokens (information) during LLM inference."
  - Anthropic presents context engineering as the natural progression of prompt engineering. Prompt engineering covers writing and organizing instructions, especially system prompts. Context engineering covers everything else that enters the window, including tools, MCP, external data and message history.
  - The work is iterative because curation happens every time the agent decides what to pass to the model, not once.
- **Google, "Architecting efficient context-aware multi-agent framework for production" (Hangfei Lin, ADK), 4 December 2025.** — [Google Developers Blog, 4 Dec 2025](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/architecting-efficient-context-aware-multi-agent-framework-for-production/)
  - Google defines context engineering as "treating context as a first-class system with its own architecture, lifecycle, and constraints."
  - It describes context as a compiled view. Sessions, memory and artifacts are the source state, an ordered pipeline of processors compiles them, and the working context is the per-call view sent to the model.
  - Its principles are to separate storage from presentation, to build context through explicit transformations ("named, ordered processors, not ad-hoc string concatenation"), and to scope by default ("Every model call and sub-agent sees the minimum context required").
  - It describes four tiers: working context, session event log, memory and artifacts. Artifacts are large files that the agent references through handles.
  - Stable prefixes go first to improve cache hits. In handoffs, prior "Assistant" messages are rewritten so that a new agent does not mistake them for its own outputs.
  - The post reports no benchmark numbers.
- **Drew Breunig, "The State of Context Engineering", 24 October 2026.** Breunig frames the field as moving from prompt engineering (2023) through the long-context promise (2024) and context engineering (2025) to harness engineering (2026). Harness engineering means designing the scaffold, orchestration and tooling of an agentic application. — [dbreunig.com, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.dbreunig.com/2026/10/24/the-state-of-context-engineering.html)
  - His thesis is that "Our output is the product of the context and the weights." He describes the job as deciding what to make deterministic in the harness and what to leave to the probabilistic model.

### Inferences
- The definitions agree on three points. Context is everything the model sees, not only the prompt. Context is assembled dynamically at every step. Its quality is the main lever the engineer controls once the weights are fixed.
- The Cognition and Anthropic positions on multi-agent systems differ in emphasis more than they conflict. Both accept that isolated subagents lose shared context. Cognition concludes that agents should avoid parallel writers, and Anthropic uses subagents for read-heavy, parallelizable research where each subagent returns a condensed summary.

### Gaps
- I did not locate a primary OpenAI publication that defines "context engineering" in these terms. OpenAI's "A practical guide to building agents" (2025) and its Agents SDK documentation on session memory and trimming may be relevant, but I did not fetch or verify them within this task's budget.
- I could not verify the exact date of Karpathy's post from a primary source, because secondary sources disagree by a few days.

## 2. What is the evidence on context rot and long-context degradation?

### Takeaway
Measured results consistently show that accuracy falls as input length grows, well before the advertised context limit. The decline is steeper when the task needs inference rather than literal matching, when distractors are present, and when the relevant fact sits in the middle of the input. Top frontier models improved on some benchmarks through 2025 and 2026. No source shows that the degradation has disappeared.

### Cited Findings
- **"Lost in the Middle" (Liu et al., Stanford and others; arXiv July 2023, TACL).** This is a measured result. The paper tested multi-document question answering and key-value retrieval. Performance "is often highest when relevant information occurs at the beginning or end of the input context" and drops when the information sits in the middle, which gives a U-shaped curve. The effect also appears in models built for long contexts. — [arXiv 2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)
- **RULER (Hsieh et al., NVIDIA; April 2024, COLM 2024).** This is a measured result. RULER has 13 tasks covering retrieval with varied needle types and counts, multi-hop tracing and aggregation, and it was run on 17 long-context models. Models score nearly perfectly on vanilla needle-in-a-haystack, but almost all show large drops as length grows. All of them claim 32K or more tokens of context, yet "only half of them can maintain satisfactory performance at the length of 32K." — [arXiv 2404.06654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654)
- **Databricks Mosaic Research, long-context RAG study (2024).** This is a measured result. The study ran more than 2,000 experiments on 13 open and commercial LLMs across four RAG datasets. Retrieving more documents generally helps, but most models degrade beyond a certain context size. Llama-3.1-405b starts to decline at about 32K tokens and GPT-4-0125-preview at about 64K, and only a few models stay consistent across all datasets. The authors also observed failure behaviors at long context, such as refusing on copyright grounds or summarizing the context instead of answering, and they attribute these to insufficient long-context post-training. — [Databricks blog](https://www.databricks.com/blog/long-context-rag-performance-llms); a follow-up covers o1 and Gemini: [Databricks blog](https://www.databricks.com/blog/long-context-rag-capabilities-openai-o1-and-google-gemini)
- **NoLiMa (Modarressi et al., Adobe Research and LMU Munich; February 2025, ICML 2025).** This is a measured result. NoLiMa is a needle-in-a-haystack test in which the needle and the question share minimal lexical overlap, so the model must infer a latent association. Of 13 models that claim at least 128K context, 11 fall below 50% of their short-context baseline at 32K tokens. GPT-4o falls from 99.3% to 69.7%. Reasoning models and chain-of-thought prompting do not remove the drop. — [arXiv 2502.05167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167)
- **Chroma, "Context Rot: How Increasing Input Tokens Impacts LLM Performance" (Kelly Hong, Anton Troynikov, Jeff Huber; 14 July 2025).** This is a measured result from a vendor technical report with a published method. It tested 18 models: 5 from Anthropic, 7 from OpenAI, 3 from Google and 3 from Alibaba. — [Chroma Research, 14 Jul 2025](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)
  - The tasks were deliberately simple, and performance still was not uniform across input lengths.
  - Performance degraded faster as input grew when the needle and question had lower semantic similarity. The tested similarity ranges were 0.445 to 0.775 for the Paul Graham essays and 0.521 to 0.829 for the arXiv set.
  - Even one topically related distractor reduced accuracy relative to the needle-only baseline, and four distractors reduced it further. The distractors did not all have the same effect. Claude models tended to abstain, and GPT models produced the most confident hallucinations.
  - Haystacks with shuffled sentences outperformed coherent ones in all 18 models. The authors wrote that "structural coherence consistently hurts model performance."
  - On LongMemEval (306 prompts), focused prompts of about 300 tokens scored significantly higher than full prompts of about 113K tokens for every model. Claude showed the largest gap, mostly because it abstained when the input was ambiguous.
  - In the repeated-words replication task, performance fell with length, and some models produced random words somewhere between 500 and 5,000 words.
  - The authors conclude that how information is presented matters as much as whether it is present.
- **Fiction.LiveBench (fiction.live; 2025 to 2026).** This is a measured result, but the leaderboard is maintained by a company rather than published as a peer-reviewed study. Epoch AI describes it as 36 questions over 30 stories that require theory of mind and inference across long fiction, and it is harder than retrieval tests. — [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/fictionlivebench)
  - In 2025, o3 reached 100% at 128K tokens and Gemini 2.5 Pro reached 90.6%. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openais-o3-achieves-near-perfect-performance-on-long-context-benchmark/)
  - Third-party aggregators list GPT-5 and o3-pro at 97.2% at 16K, but their snapshot dates conflict and the scores vary between mirrors. — [datalearner](https://www.datalearner.com/en/benchmarks/fiction-livebench); [llmrun](https://llmrun.dev/benchmark/fiction-livebench)
- **Gemini 2.5 Pro technical report and Pokémon agent (2025).** This is a practitioner observation, not a controlled measurement. Once the context grew "significantly beyond 100k tokens," the agent tended to repeat actions from its history instead of forming new plans. — cited in [Breunig, 22 Jun 2025](https://www.dbreunig.com/2025/06/22/how-contexts-fail-and-how-to-fix-them.html) and [Breunig, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.dbreunig.com/2026/10/24/the-state-of-context-engineering.html)
- **Anthropic's mechanism explanation (29 September 2025).** This is a vendor explanation, not a measurement. Anthropic attributes context rot to the n² pairwise attention relationships in transformers and to training data in which shorter sequences dominate. Models therefore have less experience with long-range dependencies, and attention is "stretched thin" as context grows. Anthropic describes the result as a gradual performance gradient rather than a hard cliff. — [Anthropic Engineering, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- **LOCA-bench (arXiv, February 2026).** This benchmark evaluates language agents under controllable and extreme context growth. I found it in search results but did not read its numbers. — [arXiv 2602.07962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07962)

### Inferences
- Effective context length, meaning the length at which a model still performs well, is consistently shorter than the advertised window. Several independent studies (RULER, NoLiMa, Databricks) place the first significant drop for many 2024 and 2025 models at around 32K tokens.
- Vanilla needle-in-a-haystack scores overstate long-context capability, because they reward literal matching. Benchmarks that require inference, aggregation or reasoning across distractors show much larger drops.
- Strong 2025 and 2026 frontier results on Fiction.LiveBench suggest that the top models narrowed the gap on some tasks. The Chroma and NoLiMa results, together with practitioner reports such as the Pokémon agent and Breunig's habit of resetting at about 200K tokens, indicate that agents still benefit from keeping context small.

### Gaps
- I could not access the official fiction.live leaderboard, so the current 2026 Fiction.LiveBench numbers rest on third-party mirrors whose snapshot dates conflict.
- The search did not surface any 2026 replication of the Chroma context-rot study on current-generation models (for example Claude Opus 4.6 or GPT-5 class models).

## 3. What are the named context failure modes and their mitigations?

### Takeaway
Drew Breunig's June 2025 taxonomy names four failure modes: poisoning, distraction, confusion and clash. It pairs them with six mitigations: RAG, tool loadout, quarantine, pruning, summarization and offloading. LangChain's Write/Select/Compress/Isolate framework covers the same mitigations as four operations. In October 2026 Breunig added "fighting the weights" and "context inertia" as further failure modes. He also kept the six tactics, saying they still hold.

### Cited Findings
- **Failure modes from "How Long Contexts Fail" (Breunig, 22 June 2025).** — [dbreunig.com, 22 Jun 2025](https://www.dbreunig.com/2025/06/22/how-contexts-fail-and-how-to-fix-them.html)
  - *Context poisoning* happens when a hallucination or other error enters the context and is then referenced repeatedly. In the Gemini 2.5 Pokémon agent, a poisoned goals section led the agent to pursue impossible objectives.
  - *Context distraction* happens when the context grows so long that the model over-focuses on it and neglects what it learned in training. The Gemini agent began repeating past actions beyond 100K tokens. Breunig also cites the Databricks finding that Llama 3.1 405b's correctness falls from about 32K tokens.
  - *Context confusion* happens when superfluous content, such as too many tools, degrades the response. On the Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard, every model performed worse when given more than one tool. A quantized Llama 3.1 8b failed with 46 tools and succeeded with 19, even though both fit well within its 16K window. These are measured results from the cited papers.
  - *Context clash* happens when new information conflicts with information already in the context. In the Microsoft and Salesforce "sharded prompts" study, splitting a benchmark prompt across several turns caused an average 39% drop in performance, and OpenAI o3 fell from 98.1 to 64.1. The authors attribute the drop to early wrong attempts staying in the context. This is a measured result.
- **Mitigations from "How to Fix Your Context" (Breunig, 26 June 2025).** — [dbreunig.com, 26 Jun 2025](https://www.dbreunig.com/2025/06/26/how-to-fix-your-context.html)
  - *RAG* means adding only the relevant information to the context. Breunig argues that large windows do not remove the need for it, because a "junk drawer" context still affects the response.
  - *Tool loadout* means selecting only the tool definitions a task needs. In the RAG-MCP study with DeepSeek-v3, tool descriptions began to overlap above 30 tools and the model almost always failed above 100. Retrieving fewer than 30 tools gave up to three times better tool-selection accuracy. In the "Less is More" study, an LLM tool recommender plus semantic search improved Llama 3.1 8b on the Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard by 44%, and dynamic selection saved 18% in power and 77% in speed. These are measured results from the papers.
  - *Context quarantine* means isolating work in separate threads or subagents. In Anthropic's multi-agent research system, an Opus 4 lead with Sonnet 4 subagents outperformed a single Opus 4 agent by 90.2% on an internal evaluation. This is a vendor claim from an internal evaluation.
  - *Context pruning* means removing irrelevant content. The Provence pruner, about 1.75 GB, removed 95% of a Wikipedia article while keeping the part relevant to the question. This is an anecdotal demonstration.
  - *Context summarization* means compacting accumulated context, which addresses distraction beyond about 100K tokens. The supporting evidence is anecdotal.
  - *Context offloading* means storing information outside the context, for example in a scratchpad. Anthropic's "think" tool, used with a domain-specific prompt, improved results by up to 54% on a specialized-agent benchmark (τ-bench). This is a vendor-reported benchmark result.
- **LangChain's Write/Select/Compress/Isolate framework (2 July 2025).** — [LangChain, 2 Jul 2025](https://www.langchain.com/blog/context-engineering-for-agents)
  - *Write* means saving context outside the window. Examples are scratchpads, such as Anthropic's multi-agent researcher saving its plan to memory before context is truncated at 200K tokens, and long-term memories (Reflexion, Generative Agents, and the auto-generated memories in ChatGPT, Cursor and Windsurf).
  - *Select* means pulling relevant context into the window. Examples are reading scratchpads, loading procedural memory such as CLAUDE.md and rules files, and choosing episodic or semantic memories. LangChain notes a failure in which ChatGPT injected a user's location into an image unexpectedly. Running RAG over tool descriptions gave about three times better tool selection in the cited papers. Windsurf combines grep, knowledge graphs and re-ranking for code retrieval, because embedding search becomes less reliable as a codebase grows.
  - *Compress* means keeping only the tokens the task needs. Claude Code auto-compacts at 95% of the window. Other approaches are recursive or hierarchical summarization, summarizing token-heavy tool outputs, Cognition's fine-tuned summarizer at agent handoffs, and trimming through heuristics or trained pruners such as Provence.
  - *Isolate* means splitting context across separate units. Examples are multi-agent systems (OpenAI Swarm, Anthropic's researcher), sandboxes (the HuggingFace CodeAgent keeps heavy objects as variables), and LangGraph state schemas that expose only chosen fields to the model. Anthropic reported that multi-agent systems can use up to 15 times more tokens than chat.
- **New failure modes (Breunig, 24 October 2026).** — [dbreunig.com, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.dbreunig.com/2026/10/24/the-state-of-context-engineering.html); [Context Inertia, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.dbreunig.com/2026/10/14/context-inertia.html); [Don't Fight the Weights, 11 Nov 2025](https://www.dbreunig.com/2025/11/11/don-t-fight-the-weights.html)
  - *Fighting the weights* happens "when the model doesn't do what you ask because you're working against what it was trained to do." Breunig's examples are Playwright tool-call errors in smaller models and the repeated capitalized pleas for parallel tool calls in Claude Code's system prompt.
  - *Context inertia* happens when models "hyper-focus on a hypothesis, subtask, or goal in their context, and keep pursuing it after they should stop."
  - Breunig expects long-running personal agents to accumulate conflicting memories and skills. He argues that agents will need strategic forgetting and says this problem is unsolved.
  - He cites Camassa and Shiller, "Do as I Say, Not as I Do". When contradictory French examples were added to an instruction to answer in Russian, instruction-following fell as the examples grew from 1 to 50 turns. Gemma 3 12B followed the instruction less than half the time after only a few examples, and Opus 4.6 held up longer but was also affected. This is a measured result. — [arXiv 2605.20382](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20382v3)
  - He cites "Scaling Discovery Requires Forgetting" (Tan, UC Berkeley). On the AHC014 task, a team of four agents with forgetting scored 1,144,695, four parallel agents scored 1,007,073, and a single agent scored 961,136. This is a measured result. — [arXiv 2610.20563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20563)
  - He cites "Fork-and-Flush" (Cai and Ziakas, Microsoft Research). Scores plateaued after early gains, with final scores between 68.3 and 100 across eight runs, as the agents made smaller and smaller changes. This is a measured result. — [arXiv 2610.07447](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07447v1)
  - He says that one enterprise team was loading 600 skills per engineer, which he treats as a tool-loadout problem. This is an anecdote.
  - His rule of thumb is to start resetting context at about 200K tokens.
- **Manus mitigations for clash and drift (18 July 2025).** Manus restricts tools by masking logits instead of removing them, recites its plan in todo.md, keeps errors in context, and varies serialization to avoid few-shot ruts. — [Manus, 18 Jul 2025](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)

### Inferences
- Each failure mode maps to a mitigation. Poisoning calls for validation, pruning or quarantine. Distraction calls for summarization or compaction. Confusion calls for tool loadout and RAG. Clash calls for pruning stale turns, isolating conflicting work, and not splitting requirements across turns. Inertia calls for periodic context resets and planned forgetting.
- Manus advises keeping errors in context, while the poisoning and clash evidence suggests removing wrong content. Both can hold at once. Recorded failures with their error messages help the model avoid repeating a mistake. Unflagged wrong conclusions presented as facts poison the context.

### Gaps
- Most mitigation numbers come from individual papers or vendor evaluations and have not been replicated independently. The 90.2% multi-agent gain is an Anthropic internal evaluation.
- I did not open the 2026 arXiv papers that Breunig cites. Their numbers above come from his talk transcript.

## 4. What is the attention budget / smallest high-signal token set principle, and what "right altitude" guidance exists?

### Takeaway
Anthropic treats context as a finite resource with diminishing marginal returns. Its guiding principle is to find "the smallest possible set of high-signal tokens that maximize the likelihood of some desired outcome." It recommends writing system prompts at a "right altitude," between brittle if-then logic and vague guidance. Google's "scope by default" principle and Manus's file-system offloading follow the same logic.

### Cited Findings
- **Attention budget.** Anthropic writes that LLMs, like humans with limited working memory, have an "attention budget" that every new token depletes. This creates a need for careful curation. — [Anthropic Engineering, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- **Guiding principle.** Good context engineering means finding "the smallest possible set of high-signal tokens that maximize the likelihood of some desired outcome." — [Anthropic, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- **Right altitude for system prompts.** The prompt should sit in a "Goldilocks zone" between two failures. One failure is hardcoding complex, brittle logic, which makes the agent fragile and hard to maintain. The other is vague, high-level guidance that assumes shared context the model does not have. The prompt should be specific enough to guide behavior and flexible enough to give strong heuristics. — [Anthropic, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
  - Anthropic suggests organizing sections such as background, instructions, tool guidance and output description with XML tags or Markdown headers.
  - Its approach is to start with a minimal prompt on the best model, then add instructions and examples in response to observed failures. Minimal does not necessarily mean short.
- **Tools.** Tools should be token-efficient, "self-contained, robust to error, and extremely clear" about their intended use, with little overlap between them. A common failure is a bloated tool set with ambiguous decision points. Anthropic states that if a human engineer cannot say which tool to use in a given situation, an agent cannot be expected to do better. — [Anthropic, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- **Examples.** Anthropic recommends a curated set of diverse, canonical examples rather than a laundry list of edge-case rules, describing examples as the "pictures" worth a thousand words to an LLM. — [Anthropic, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- **Just-in-time retrieval.** The agent keeps lightweight identifiers, such as file paths, stored queries and links, and loads data at runtime through tools instead of pre-loading everything with embedding retrieval. A hybrid approach often works best, and the choice depends on how dynamic the content is. Claude Code is the example, using CLAUDE.md up front plus glob and grep on demand. — [Anthropic, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- **Long-horizon techniques.** — [Anthropic, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
  - *Compaction* summarizes a near-full window and starts a new one with the summary. Tool-result clearing is the lightest form. Anthropic advises tuning for recall first and then for precision. In the Claude Code example, the agent keeps the five most recently accessed files after compaction.
  - *Structured note-taking* means agentic memory outside the window, such as NOTES.md or a to-do list. In the Claude Pokémon example, notes persisted across resets over 1,234 steps. Anthropic released a memory tool in public beta with Sonnet 4.5.
  - *Sub-agent architectures* give each subagent a clean window. A subagent explores tens of thousands of tokens and returns a condensed summary of 1,000 to 2,000 tokens to the lead agent.
- **Google's equivalent principle.** "Every model call and sub-agent sees the minimum context required." Agents reach for more information through tools, and large data stays behind artifact handles. — [Google Developers Blog, 4 Dec 2025](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/architecting-efficient-context-aware-multi-agent-framework-for-production/)
- **Measured support for minimal context.** In Chroma's LongMemEval experiment, focused prompts of about 300 tokens outperformed full prompts of about 113K tokens on every model tested. — [Chroma, 14 Jul 2025](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)

### Inferences
- The smallest-high-signal-set principle is consistent with the measured evidence in Section 2. Each added token can introduce distraction or a distractor, and longer inputs lower accuracy even on simple tasks.
- "Minimal" refers to signal density, not brevity. Anthropic's statement that minimal does not mean short, together with Breunig's "don't fight the weights" warning about repeated all-caps instructions, suggests that clear, well-structured guidance does better than terse or repetitive guidance.

### Gaps
- Anthropic's right-altitude guidance is qualitative. I found no controlled study that measures how prompt altitude affects agent success.

## 5. How has thinking evolved from 2025 to late 2026?

### Takeaway
Thinking moved in three steps. In mid-2025 the field renamed prompt engineering to context engineering (Lutke, Karpathy, Cognition, LangChain, Manus). In late 2025 it codified principles (Anthropic, Google ADK). In 2026 it shifted toward "harness engineering," where models are trained to manage their own context and the open problems are long-running memory, forgetting and context inertia. Vendor surveys report broad agreement that context, rather than model capability, is the main cause of agent failure, but they are not independent measurements.

### Cited Findings
- **Timeline of the term.** — [Breunig, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.dbreunig.com/2026/10/24/the-state-of-context-engineering.html)
  - 2023 was the year of prompt engineering.
  - In 2024 context windows grew from about 128K to 1M tokens with Gemini 1.5, and Llama 4 Scout later advertised 10M. This was the "RAG is dead" moment. Breunig comments that "long contexts let you fit in more information, but they also let you fit in more errors."
  - In 2025 attention moved to dynamic context assembly, which became context engineering.
  - In 2026 the focus became the harness, meaning the scaffold, orchestration and tooling around the model. Model training moved from "fit everything in the context" toward "teach the model to drive the harness and manage its own context."
- **Trend toward resetting context instead of only compacting it.** Breunig describes Anthropic's Memento setup, in which the agent writes notes about every 90 minutes and the context is then cleared while the environment persists. He keeps asking practitioners, "Have you tried resetting your context?" The forgetting and Fork-and-Flush results above are the measured support he cites. — [Breunig, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.dbreunig.com/2026/10/24/the-state-of-context-engineering.html)
- **Longer task horizons.** Breunig cites METR task-length figures of 3.4 hours for GPT-5, 6.4 hours for Gemini 3.1 and 12.0 hours for Opus 4.6. Tasks of this length make context management across resets central to the work. These are measured results, but I have only Breunig's citation of them and did not verify them on METR's site. — [Breunig, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.dbreunig.com/2026/10/24/the-state-of-context-engineering.html)

### Inferences
- The six mitigation tactics have stayed stable from June 2025 to October 2026. What has changed is who applies them. In 2025 the harness applied them deterministically. By 2026 models are increasingly trained to apply them through tools such as memory files, compaction and subagents.
- The 2026 problems involve time more than size. Inertia, accumulated memories and the need to forget become important once agents run for hours or days, whereas the 2025 problems were mostly about the size of a single window.
